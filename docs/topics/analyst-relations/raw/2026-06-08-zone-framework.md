Keith is transitioning from Zone 2 to Zone 1 at work. This created the opportunity to review and templatize MQ season materials, which becomes both a fractional AR offering and anchor content for Aspirationalist.

## Zone definitions

**Zone 2 — MQ Season**
The high-stakes window of proving your work to the analyst firm. Includes:
- Pre-season prep (a few weeks before welcome packet)
- Welcome packet
- Demo and questionnaire
- Final review
- Publication
- Post-season (activities directly after publication)

**Zone 1 — Always-on AR**
All the time — fully overlaps with Zone 2 but gets deprioritized when Zone 2 is active because of the workload. Two parallel tracks:

*AR's job in Zone 1:*
- Building analyst relationships continuously
- Gathering market signals and intelligence
- Telling the story — keeping analysts current

*The rest of the org's job in Zone 1:*
- Running the business: product strategy, roadmap, GTM, sales, implementation, customer feedback
- This is execution — not AR's job, but what AR depends on having something real to show

## The core insight

AR is an intelligence function, not a megaphone.

The failure mode: treating AR as pure outbound ("tell our story") while ignoring the inbound half — analyst signals that should feed business strategy.

The loop:
AR intelligence in → business thesis → roadmap → execution → product → AR has something real to show → analyst placement

Zone 2 performance is determined by Zone 1 execution. By the time the welcome packet arrives, the score is mostly already written.

Most companies treat Zone 1 like optional prep and scramble in Zone 2. The real differentiator is companies where AR was actually influencing decisions throughout Zone 1.

## The thesis this points toward

Most AR content is tactical ("how to nail your MQ submission"). The differentiated angle is strategic: "your MQ result was determined 18 months ago — here's why."
