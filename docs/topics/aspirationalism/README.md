# Aspirationalism

Aspirationalism is the evolving methodology at the center of the Aspirationalist Project.

Keith Andes is developing and defining it through this project. The definition is intentionally unfinished and should improve as the ideas are articulated, tested, challenged, and applied.

## Working definition

Aspirationalism is the optimistic belief that humanity has the capacity to influence the future and build a better one, paired with the desire to contribute productively toward that future as individuals and together.

This is a working definition. It is intended to improve over time.

## Current shape

The methodology currently includes five connected ideas:

1. **Optimism:** A better future is possible.
2. **Agency:** Humanity has the capacity to influence what the future becomes.
3. **Contribution:** Individuals can choose to contribute productively to that future.
4. **Collective capacity:** People working together can accomplish more than isolated effort.
5. **Momentum:** Belief should lead to action. Start building. LFG.

The current definition describes the mindset and intent. The practical principles, methods, and standards that make Aspirationalism a complete methodology still need to be developed.

## Relationship to the Aspirationalist Project

Aspirationalism is the methodology being developed.

The Aspirationalist Project is the body of work used to define, test, document, improve, and share it.

## What we're building toward

This topic should eventually contain:

- a clearer and more durable definition;
- foundational beliefs and principles;
- practical methods for individual and collective contribution;
- examples of Aspirationalism applied to technology, business, systems of work, and human progress;
- tensions, limitations, and failure modes;
- frameworks that make the methodology usable; and
- artifacts that help other people understand and practice it.

## Content maturity

`raw → frameworks → assets`

- **raw/** contains working definitions, source language, unresolved beliefs, examples, and early models.
- **frameworks/** contains deliberately developed principles, methods, and named concepts.
- **assets/** contains finished outputs designed to explain or help people apply Aspirationalism.

The current definition belongs in raw material until Keith deliberately approves a more stable version.

## Open questions

- Is Aspirationalism best understood as a mindset, worldview, methodology, ideology, or some combination?
- Which principles are essential to the definition?
- What practices turn optimism and agency into productive contribution?
- What standards separate useful optimism from wishful thinking?
- How should individual responsibility and collective coordination relate?
- How should the methodology address tradeoffs, harm, uncertainty, and competing ideas of a better future?
- What does it look like when Aspirationalism is successfully practiced?
