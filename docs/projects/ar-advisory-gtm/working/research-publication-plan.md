# AR research-publication and advisory validation plan

Status: Working plan  
Updated: 2026-07-30

## Project objective

Use a practitioner publication to create useful, trusted conversations with AR leaders. Use optional inquiry-style advisory sessions and direct follow-up to learn where Keith's MQ perspective creates repeatable value. Produce two publishable articles and enough evidence to decide whether to design a paid beta.

## Working research question

**What do AR leaders wish executives understood about the Magic Quadrant?**

Supporting question:

Where do executive expectations create friction, wasted effort, or poor decisions during the MQ cycle, and what should companies do differently?

## Phase 1: Two-person pilot

### Outcome

Determine whether the article angle produces specific insight, participant referrals, and live situations where an optional advisory session would be useful.

### Participants

Start with two AR practitioners Keith already knows and believes will give candid feedback.

Selection criteria:

- direct responsibility for Gartner or MQ work;
- enough experience to discuss executive expectations;
- willingness to speak candidly;
- a relationship strong enough for an informal request; and
- different company or program contexts when possible.

### Initial outreach

The invitation asks only for the article interview.

> Hey [Name], I'm working on a piece about what AR leaders wish executives understood about the Magic Quadrant, especially where expectations create wasted effort or make it harder to run the program effectively.
>
> I thought you'd potentially have some cool insights that would make a great contribution to the article. Would you be up for a casual 25-minute conversation?
>
> I'm happy to keep anything anonymous or let you review attributed quotes before I use them.

Personalize the second sentence with a specific reason the person is relevant whenever possible.

### Pilot success questions

After two interviews, review:

- Did the topic produce specific stories and useful insight?
- Did the conversations go beyond generic AR commentary?
- Did either participant identify another person to interview?
- Did either participant have a live issue suited to the optional advisory session?
- Did the interview reveal a stronger or narrower article angle?
- Did the participant experience feel useful enough to continue?

If the topic remains generic, revise the question before expanding.

## Phase 2: Practitioner interviews

Expand to approximately five to eight interviews when the pilot produces useful evidence.

### Interview structure

#### 1. Context

- What do you own around Gartner and the MQ?
- How experienced is your company with the process?
- Who participates internally, and who has the strongest expectations?

#### 2. Executive expectations

- What do executives believe the MQ measures or accomplishes?
- Where does their understanding differ from yours?
- What is the hardest MQ conversation you have to have internally?
- Can you walk me through a recent example?

#### 3. Priorities and effort

- Where does the company spend the most time during the MQ cycle?
- Which activities create the most useful business value?
- Where do you think the company overinvests or loses the intent behind the work?
- What pressure shapes those choices?

#### 4. AR influence and support

- Where do you feel least equipped or supported?
- What do you wish someone had helped you understand earlier?
- Where would an experienced outside perspective be useful?
- What do you wish product, marketing, or executive leaders understood about your role?

#### 5. Publication close

- What is the single thing you wish SaaS executives understood about the Magic Quadrant?
- Is there anything important I did not ask?
- How may I use your contribution: attributed, quote approval required, anonymous, or background only?

### Referral question

Ask before mentioning the advisory session:

> Is there anyone else in AR you think I should talk to about this topic?

Capture the name, reason, and whether the participant is willing to make an introduction.

## Optional advisory session

After closing the interview and asking for referrals, offer the session separately:

> As a thank-you for your time today, I'm happy to offer a separate free advisory session on any MQ or AR issue where my former Gartner perspective might be useful. Completely optional. If something comes to mind, send me a little context and I'll come prepared.

### Preparation

If accepted, ask only for what is needed to understand the live issue:

- What decision or situation do you want to work through?
- Why does it matter now?
- Who is involved?
- What have you already tried or considered?
- What constraints or sensitivities should I understand?
- Is there a document, excerpt, or non-confidential background item I should review?

Keep article material and private advisory information in separate records.

### Inquiry-style session structure

Suggested length: 45 minutes.

1. Clarify the situation, decision, and desired outcome.
2. Ask questions until the underlying issue is clear.
3. Share the interpretation Keith believes matters most.
4. Pressure-test the participant's options and assumptions.
5. Recommend the next action or decision.
6. Ask the participant what they plan to use.
7. Agree on a follow-up date.

The session does not need a standardized deliverable during the early pilot. A short written recap may be provided when it helps the participant act and gives the project evidence about useful outputs.

### Impact follow-up

Follow up approximately one to two weeks later:

- What advice or framing did you use?
- What action did you take?
- What happened?
- Did the advice change a decision, priority, message, or allocation?
- What remained difficult?
- Would one short follow-up call help?

One free follow-up call may be offered when it helps complete the learning loop.

## Phase 3: Evidence review

Use `validation-tracker.md` after every interaction.

After every three interviews, review the pattern record:

- Which problems repeated independently?
- Which situations carried urgency or executive consequences?
- Which participant language repeated?
- Which advisory recommendations created action?
- Which outputs did participants request?
- Which signals reflect politeness or content interest?
- Which signals suggest continuing service value?
- What should change in the interview or advisory approach?

## Phase 5: Distribution research and pitching

Complete this task before publishing the full articles on LinkedIn or another public channel.

### Build the target list

Research relevant:

- AR practitioner organizations and communities;
- product marketing organizations and publications;
- B2B marketing and PR publications;
- B2B SaaS executive publications and communities;
- podcasts;
- webinar and virtual-event programs;
- panels and roundtables; and
- adjacent AR firms with meaningful communities or distribution.

For each target, capture:

- audience;
- editorial fit;
- exclusivity or originality requirements;
- accepted formats;
- editor, community lead, or producer;
- expected lead time;
- pitch angle; and
- priority order.

### Pitch sequence

1. Finish the strongest draft and a short findings summary.
2. Pitch the strongest exclusive target first.
3. Offer the article plus alternative formats: podcast, webinar, panel, roundtable, or research discussion.
4. Give a specific response window.
5. Move to the next outlet when the window closes.
6. Self-publish when external placement no longer creates enough incremental value.

### Pitch template

> I've completed a practitioner research piece based on interviews with AR leaders about where executive expectations create friction during the Magic Quadrant process.
>
> I thought the findings could fit your audience and would be happy to share the draft for consideration. I'm also open to using the research for a podcast, webinar, panel, or practitioner discussion if another format is more useful.
>
> I'm planning to place or publish the piece by [date]. Please let me know by [response date] if you would like to review it.

## Phase 6: Paid-beta decision

A paid beta can be designed when the project has evidence for all of the following:

- a repeated buyer and triggering situation;
- a consequential problem;
- an immediate outcome participants value;
- a repeatable method or advisory mechanism;
- a bounded engagement or deliverable;
- a likely economic buyer; and
- at least one credible continuation or willingness-to-pay signal.

If the publication succeeds while advisory sessions produce no meaningful action or continuation signals, treat that as content validation and reconsider the service path.
