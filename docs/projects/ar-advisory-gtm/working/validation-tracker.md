## Three-interview retrospective

Run after every three completed interviews.

### Content

- Is the article question producing specific stories?
- Which findings are genuinely surprising or useful?
- Are participants referring relevant peers?
- Is the likely article becoming clearer?

### Problem

- Which situations repeated independently?
- Which consequences carried real weight?
- Where did urgency appear?
- Which language should replace Keith's current generic wording?

### Service

- Which participants accepted the advisory session?
- Which recommendations led to an action?
- What did participants actually use?
- Which outcomes can be observed rather than inferred?
- What advisory work appeared repeatable?

### Offer

- Did anyone request continued help?
- Did anyone refer a peer because of the advisory value?
- Did an economic buyer enter the conversation?
- Did willingness to pay appear without Keith forcing the question?
- Is there enough evidence to describe a bounded beta?

### Failure and bias check

- Are positive comments being mistaken for action?
- Are article interviews being mistaken for buyer discovery?
- Are referrals driven by the publication topic or advisory value?
- Is Keith avoiding advisory follow-up because content work feels easier?
- Are participants accepting free help without a consequential problem?
- Is one unusually enthusiastic participant distorting the pattern?
- Is the project collecting private details that cannot support public research?

## Evidence interpretation rules

### Content validation supports

- continuing or refining the research;
- writing the article;
- expanding distribution; and
- building Keith's body of work.

It does not establish a service or paid offer.

### Problem validation supports

- narrowing the target situation;
- improving the research question;
- selecting advisory cases; and
- forming a problem hypothesis.

It does not establish that Keith's intervention works.

### Service validation supports

- defining a repeatable advisory mechanism;
- identifying useful questions and outputs;
- narrowing the free pre-offer; and
- testing a more structured engagement.

It requires observable use or change after the advice.

### Offer validation supports

- scoping a paid beta;
- identifying the buyer;
- testing pricing or commercial structure; and
- developing a route to market.

The strongest signals involve requests, introductions, commitments, or payment rather than compliments.
