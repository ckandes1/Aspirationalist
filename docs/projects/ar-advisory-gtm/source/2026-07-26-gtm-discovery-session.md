# AR advisory GTM discovery session

Status: Project source, not canonical guidance  
Captured: 2026-07-26  
Potential direction: Buyer research, free advisory discovery, a paid AR/MQ offer, and a later content-led campaign

## Source purpose

This note preserves the initial interview and the reasoning that changed the project sequence.

The session began as campaign planning. It moved upstream after Keith recognized that he could not responsibly design the campaign, free asset, or paid offer without first understanding what he could repeatedly help a real client do.

The current conclusion is intentionally incomplete: begin with buyer research and a small number of free advisory engagements, use the work to discover the repeatable value, then define the paid offer and campaign.

## Starting situation

Keith originally wanted an end-to-end marketing campaign built primarily around LinkedIn content, with selective Substack reuse, light private follow-up, and possible paid promotion of content that performed well.

The campaign needed to account for several constraints:

- no dedicated website or business email yet;
- public activity could not make an active freelance campaign obvious to the current employer;
- strong preference for inbound over conventional outbound;
- a free resource could move people beyond LinkedIn and create a more transparent funnel;
- the work should follow an Agile, Lean, MVP approach with short feedback loops;
- the first launch is one test, not a single irreversible opportunity.

The initial funnel concept was:

`useful LinkedIn content → free deeper asset → light follow-up → paid offer`

The work stalled because the paid offer and its value were still unclear.

## GTM order that emerged

The session established this lean sequence:

`market opportunity → buyer problem → desired outcome → offer → evidence → message → route to market → campaign → learning`

Keith recognized a recurring bad habit learned from asset-first GTM work: beginning with campaign materials, sales slides, personas, or messaging before making the underlying market, buyer, product, and strategic choices.

The immediate implication was to pause campaign planning and investigate the offer.

## Market opportunity observations

Keith believes AR teams commonly understand Gartner and the MQ in principle, but lack the experience of having been inside Gartner and watching the evaluation process directly.

His wording:

> "The AR people know it, for the most part, but they know it by essence. They haven't been there to see it, so they don't know the nuance and the details."

He also described AR teams as feeling insufficiently equipped for the arguments they need to make internally.

The recurring organizational gap is between how AR understands Gartner and how executives understand it. AR may give useful strategic advice, while executives continue to treat the function primarily as a PR or placement mechanism.

Keith connected this to a broader company problem. Objectives and metrics become checklists. People pursue the measured output and lose the intent behind the work. The company can eventually lose clarity about what it built, why it exists, and what customer problem it solves. That weakens the story communicated across the company, including to analysts.

## Initial audience ordering

Keith's current audience order is:

1. Analyst Relations leaders and practitioners.
2. CEOs and CMOs.
3. Product marketing.
4. Product and strategy leaders.

Competitive intelligence and other adjacent roles may also be relevant.

The initial offer should likely speak most directly to the AR person who owns or influences MQ outcomes and needs stronger executive understanding and support.

## Buyer problem observations

The strongest prospect may feel that:

- the MQ process remains mysterious despite their experience;
- they do not understand the available levers with enough confidence;
- they cannot tell whether the company is spending time in the right areas;
- they are unsure whether the company is delivering its message well;
- Gartner may be underappreciating or misunderstanding the message;
- they have never run this situation before, or have not seen enough cycles to know the nuance;
- they need to educate executives but do not feel fully equipped to make the case;
- AR objectives, metrics, stakeholder expectations, and actual work may be misaligned.

Keith did not identify disappointment with current dot placement as the defining prospect condition. The deeper problem is uncertainty about how the system works, what can be influenced, and what deserves attention.

## Desired outcome observations

Keith found it difficult to state a generic outcome because the useful recommendation would depend on the company's situation.

The clearest emerging value was:

- review the MQ and AR program;
- identify where the team is wasting time;
- reduce effort on low-value activities;
- redirect resources toward work that matters more;
- improve current ROI from AR;
- improve the effectiveness of the function so stronger MQ performance becomes more likely over time;
- help AR communicate and defend priorities with executives.

Keith distinguished immediate value from long-term placement improvement. Moving the dot is a long game. The near-term value is greater clarity, better allocation, and a more effective program.

His raw phrasing included:

> "I think I would have to review their MQ program and tell them where they're wasting time."

> "We can probably reduce resources on dumb things."

> "It would be doing is making us more effective so that we can increase dot placement in a long time, and we can actually get better ROI now."

## Central uncertainty

Keith has substantial relevant knowledge but has never packaged and applied it as an external service.

His clearest statement of the tension was:

> "I have the knowledge that they need to be successful, but I don't know how to apply it because I've never applied it."

Trying to specify the paid offer, diagnostic phases, final deliverable, and promise before working with a real company created the feeling of spinning in circles.

Keith said:

> "I feel like I've been spinning my wheels on something I'm never going to be able to do."

> "I don't even know how we get to the stage where I have an offer to give to somebody at this rate."

The session initially responded by continuing to invent likely service components. That increased the uncertainty because the missing input was experience applying the knowledge to a client's real situation.

## Pivot to free discovery work

Keith proposed offering help for free to a small number of people so he can learn how his knowledge becomes useful in practice.

The intent is genuine collaboration and discovery. It is not a free fragment that stops at an artificial boundary unless the person pays.

Keith rejected framing it as a trial or a disguised upsell. The scope may be flexible because the point is to explore the situation, find where he can help, and learn what a future service should become.

His framing was:

> "I'm just trying to figure out how I can help people and define an offer later."

> "I'm giving you a free thing. I don't know the full scope of that thing."

The current concept is to make a limited number of free engagements available, potentially three, explain that Keith is developing a future offer, and be direct that the current work is free research and help.

The free work should create mutual value:

- the participant receives Keith's experience and perspective;
- Keith learns the buyer's situation, language, materials, decisions, and unmet needs;
- the observed patterns shape the future paid offer.

## Initial conversation questions

Keith's first question would be:

> "Tell me what your current objectives are in the role, what's going well, and critically, what's not? Why are you talking to me today?"

He would also want to understand the history and operating environment:

- How large is the AR team?
- Which analyst firms do they engage with?
- Do they have a firm or analyst tiering system?
- Which reports have they participated in recently?
- Which reports are they targeting next?
- How have prior cycles gone?
- What does the executive team think about the function and the reports?
- Do AR and executives agree on which reports matter, what placement means, and how to improve it?
- What are the team's objectives and metrics?
- How mature is the function?
- How long has the team existed?
- How long has the participant been in the role?
- Does the executive team consider the function successful?
- Which stakeholders care, which do not, and which need to care?

Layoff or reduction risk may matter when it changes urgency, behavior, or executive pressure. It is secondary to the program and stakeholder diagnosis.

## Initial materials and inputs

For an MQ-specific discussion, Keith would likely request:

- the prior year's welcome packet;
- the prior questionnaire submission;
- the current welcome packet and questionnaire when available;
- the current point in the MQ cycle;
- any retrospective or internal debrief from the prior cycle;
- a summary of what went well and what did not;
- current executive expectations, concerns, and priorities.

The first diagnostic lens would compare:

1. the team's objectives and metrics;
2. stakeholder and executive expectations; and
3. where the function actually spends its time.

The specific evaluation method still needs to be developed through real cases and the MQ Package.

## Evidence already available

Keith has several forms of credibility and early channel evidence:

- seven years inside Gartner, including analyst and management experience;
- vendor-side responsibility for MQ and Analyst Relations work;
- product marketing, positioning, competitive intelligence, and strategy experience;
- prior LinkedIn posts on Gartner and MQ topics that generated strong reach and engagement;
- three or four prior inbound requests asking whether he offered services.

The previous inbound interest is why Keith wants to repeat the content motion with an actual path for interested people to continue.

## Initial route to market

Keith expects to rebuild a body of LinkedIn content around valuable points that analysts know but often do not explain clearly.

The content advantage is his ability to explain the same underlying knowledge from the perspective of a former analyst who now works on the vendor side.

The current route may combine:

- refreshed LinkedIn content;
- new content developed from the broader body of AR and MQ knowledge;
- a free resource or low-pressure CTA;
- movement into DMs, a group, Substack, or another owned contact path;
- research interviews with relevant people who engage;
- a small number of free advisory engagements;
- a future paid offer based on what proves useful.

Keith strongly prefers inbound. He is willing to conduct targeted research outreach when it is genuinely framed as research rather than sales.

He referenced a product marketer who contacted peers to understand product-market fit for a future service, clearly stating that nothing was being sold. Keith sees that as a credible model for initial interviews.

Paid amplification and boosted posts remain possible later. They are not required for the discovery phase.

## What is currently decided

- Work upstream from the campaign.
- Preserve the Lean sequence from opportunity through learning.
- Start with buyer and offer discovery.
- Use real conversations and free help to learn how Keith's expertise creates value.
- Keep the paid offer unresolved until evidence exists.
- Keep campaign execution paused until the buyer problem and offer are clearer.
- Track the work as a project with a durable pickup point.
