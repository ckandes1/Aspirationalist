# AR Advisory GTM

## Current decision

The current route to market is an interview-led research campaign:

`AR practitioner interviews → qualitative report → open publication → voluntary subscription or question → private relationship → possible advisory work`

The report will be published ungated on a personalized Substack site. Substack will capture readers who voluntarily want future resources without withholding the current report.

## Why this is the current plan

Keith's existing LinkedIn content already demonstrates that his former-Gartner perspective can generate attention and establish authority. The missing component is a substantive destination that turns isolated posts into a coherent body of work and gives relevant readers a reason to stay connected.

Interviews come before heavier campaign posting because they create:

1. original evidence for a differentiated report;
2. a stronger asset than another opinion post;
3. insight into the problems AR practitioners actually face; and
4. material that future posts, articles, podcasts, and discussions can distribute.

The product philosophy behind the ungated decision is to choose the model that most strongly incentivizes the resource to be good. The report must earn readership, trust, subscription, and conversation through its usefulness.

## Two separate flows

### Research flow

Interview 8–10 AR practitioners and combine their internal experience with Keith's cross-vendor perspective as a former Gartner analyst.

Participants are research contributors, not consulting prospects. They should not be funneled toward paid work. Any separate advisory session offered as a thank-you remains optional and carries no sales expectation.

### Audience and customer flow

LinkedIn posts, articles, podcasts, Substack Notes, search, and participant sharing bring readers to the open report.

Readers can then choose one of two next steps:

- **Subscribe for more free resources.**
- **Bring me a question.**

Subscription preserves the relationship with readers who want more but are not ready for a direct conversation. The question path gives an AR professional or executive with an active issue a quiet way to engage. Any advisory opportunity develops privately after a real problem and fit become clear.

## CTA logic

### Subscribe for more free resources

Supporting promise:

> No useless weekly newsletter. I'll email only when I have research, an article, a practical tool, or another resource worth sending.

Subscriber communication is an irregular resource series. Useful sends may include AR/MQ research, articles, templates, diagnostics, practical tools, invitations to contribute to future research, or small peer discussions.

### Bring me a question

This should be a persistent site or menu CTA rather than a hard sell inside every article.

It must be inviting to an AR practitioner or executive who wants Keith's perspective while remaining truthfully explainable as professional discussion, research, and peer advice. The final supporting copy can be tested later. The CTA label is the current direction.

## Employer constraint

Public activity must pass the glance-and-ask test. The visible campaign is accurately about research, useful resources, professional dialogue, and thought leadership. Avoid public language such as "book a consulting call," "hire me," or "accepting clients."

Private conversations may lead to advisory work when someone voluntarily brings a real problem and wants deeper help.

## Publication and brand direction

- Publish the full report openly through Substack.
- Use `keithandes.xyz` as the likely public domain because it is easy to type and connects the expertise directly to Keith.
- Keep Aspirationalist as the broader publication or brand name.
- Treat the domain choice as a working decision. It is not a prerequisite for starting interviews.

## Immediate action plan

1. Pick five people to contact.
2. Write the outreach message.
3. Choose the video meeting platform and confirm how the meeting link, recording, and transcript will work.
4. Ping the first person and schedule the interview using that platform.
5. Prepare the interview guide before the meeting.
6. Run a short practice call. Verify the recording, transcript quality, speaker labels, and transcript export.
7. Conduct interview one.
8. Write immediate closeout notes about the interview and what was learned.
9. Combine the closeout notes, interview notes, and transcript in one record.
10. Review what would make the next interview better, then revise the guide or setup.
11. Continue toward 8–10 total interviews.

## What waits

Do not make Substack design, custom-domain setup, publication formatting, email automation, or the broader content campaign prerequisites for interviewing.

Those begin once interviews are underway and the report's substance and shape are becoming clear.
