# The Idea Murder Machine — Gate One

## Purpose
When you bring an idea to a conversation, this gate determines whether it becomes an active project or dies. Run every idea through these five questions in order. If the idea passes all five, it moves from inbox to queue — it is now a project. If it fails at any point, it is dead or shelved. Move on without guilt.

---

## The Five Questions

**1. Do I actually care about this?**
Gut check first. How excited are you about this idea? Does it have real potential — for you, for the people it would serve? If the honest answer is "not really," stop here. A perfectly testable idea you don't care about is still a waste of time.

**2. What is the idea at its simplest — and what is the artifact?**
Strip away the complexity. State the core idea in two or three sentences. No jargon, no qualifications. If you can't state it simply, you don't understand it well enough yet. Then name the artifact explicitly: what are you actually building, what form does it take, and what does someone do with it? A thesis document, a checklist, a presentation, a tool — name it. If you can't name the artifact yet, the idea is not ready to become a project.

**3. What is the load-bearing assumption?**
Every idea has one thing that, if false, kills the whole idea. Find it. It is usually the thing you are most hoping is true but haven't actually verified. Name it explicitly. This is the thing you are about to test.

**4. What does minimum viability look like?**
Forget the full vision. What is the smallest real version of this idea that would be genuinely useful to someone? Who specifically does it serve? What do they actually do differently because of it? If you can't answer those questions concretely, the idea isn't ready to become a project yet.

**5. What is the cheapest way to test the load-bearing assumption?**
Not a full build. Not months of research. What is the scrappiest thing you could do — in hours or days, not weeks — that would tell you whether the load-bearing assumption is true or false? If you can't design a cheap test, that is a red flag. Good ideas are cheap to falsify. If it passes this test, the idea becomes a project. If it fails, the idea is dead.

---

## How Claude Should Use This
When an idea is brought into a conversation, apply this gate before any planning, building, or designing begins. Ask each question explicitly. Do not let the conversation skip ahead to solution mode until all five questions have honest answers. If the idea passes, define the next concrete action before ending the conversation. If the idea fails, name it dead and move on.
